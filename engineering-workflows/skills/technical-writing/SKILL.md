# Technical Writing

Identify the reader's task and choose a structure suited to it: instructions, explanation, reference, or decision record. Lead with the outcome and use concrete, accurate language. Separate facts, assumptions, and unresolved decisions; verify commands and claims where feasible.

Use short sentences and consistent terminology. Remove filler, repetition, unsupported claims, and artificial phrasing while preserving the author's voice and necessary detail. Keep procedures ordered and actionable. Respect existing project style, accessibility, and documentation conventions.

## Choose the document's job

A tutorial helps someone learn by completing a task. A how-to helps a reader reach a specific result. Reference material describes facts for lookup. An explanation develops a concept or decision. Choose the main job before writing and put background in a separate section when it would interrupt a procedure.

Use the project's actual names for commands, flags, files, and domain concepts. For instructions, put the condition before the action and give the expected result where a reader needs confirmation. Describe a failure and recovery step when the reader can act on it. Do not invent command output or product behavior.

On the edit pass, remove repeated claims and vague modifiers. Replace abstract wording with a concrete action or result. Keep the author's voice and vary sentence length naturally; short sentences are not an excuse to omit conditions or articles.

## Unslop a draft

When the user asks to unslop writing, preserve its meaning, evidence, intended audience, and the author's voice. Inspect the whole draft before editing. Remove unsupported claims and vague attributions, or replace them with specific evidence when available. Do not turn uncertainty into certainty.

Look for stock transitions, inflated verbs, repeated sentence patterns, synonym switching, decorative headings, and conclusions that add no information. Replace vague claims with concrete actions or results. For example, change "enhances the developer experience" to the actual improvement, such as "reports the file and line that failed validation", only when the evidence supports it.

Use ordinary words, active verbs, and sentence case. Split a sentence when it carries multiple unrelated ideas, but keep conditions and qualifications needed for accuracy. Avoid imposing a single punctuation or sentence-length rule when it would damage the author's style. Read the result for tone and missing meaning before presenting it.

## Edit without changing the claim

Check that every pronoun has a clear referent and that a condition appears before the instruction it limits. Replace long noun strings with the relationship between the things they name. Keep technical terms when they are the project's established vocabulary or are more precise than a simpler phrase.

For an unslop pass, compare the edited version with the original for lost qualifications, changed numbers, missing citations, and altered tone. Avoid replacing a source's uncertainty with confident wording or removing an important exception just to shorten a sentence.
